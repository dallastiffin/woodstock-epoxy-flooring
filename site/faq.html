For most properties, yes. Winters here put bare concrete through road brine, slush and repeated freeze-thaw damage, and an unsealed slab absorbs every bit of it. Sealing the floor keeps that brine and meltwater on the surface instead of letting it soak in, and along the way the space gets brighter, easier to keep clean, and far more resistant to oil staining. A properly installed coating commonly runs 15 to 20 years, which stacks up well against patching a spalled slab every couple of winters instead.

Given the freeze-thaw pattern here, the combination that holds up best is an epoxy base coat, a broadcast of colour flakes, and a polyaspartic top coat. The epoxy bonds hard into the ground concrete and builds real thickness. The flakes hide dirt and tire marks between cleanings. The polyaspartic layer resists abrasion, cures through cooler weather, and keeps its colour under sunlight. A single-layer retail kit runs far thinner than any of that and rarely survives more than a couple of Woodstock winters.

You can, though the results seldom hold up long term. Retail kits lean on an acid wash rather than diamond grinding, and that shortcut does not open the concrete's surface enough for a coating to really grip it. Kits also go down much thinner than a professional system, often under 5 mils against the 20 to 30 mils we build up on a job here. Most kit floors around Oxford County start lifting at the door line and tire paths within two or three winters.

Foot traffic is generally fine about 12 hours after the final coat goes on, with vehicles clear to return in 24 to 48 hours. A polyaspartic top coat cures considerably faster than standard epoxy on its own, which is why it ends up on most of the jobs we run. Exact timing shifts with slab temperature and humidity during the install, and you get written cure times once the work wraps up.

The coating does not add insulation on its own, but it reads warmer underfoot than bare concrete and holds heat from an area rug or in-floor heating noticeably better than an untreated slab. Because Woodstock sits on a glacial till plain that drains slowly, we also check basement slabs for moisture before quoting, since a damp floor needs a different primer before any coating goes down.
